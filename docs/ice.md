# 🧊 ICE — Intrusion Countermeasures Electronics

BANKON's ICE tab is named for the cyberpunk concept: software that stands between an
intruder and the thing worth protecting. In BANKON, ICE is *the wall between the network
and the wallet* — CPU heat gating, the radio AIRGAP kill switch, and the forensic toolkit
(geo/IP forensics, Net Map cross-links, 18-decimal precision metrics, the shreddable
`.history` evidence trail, EtherApe live wire capture).

## Provenance of the term

- **Coined by Tom Maddox** — acknowledged both by the Jargon File and by William Gibson
  himself. Maddox later used the concept in his own fiction (e.g. "Snake Eyes", 1986).
- **Popularized by William Gibson**, first in the short story **"Burning Chrome"**
  (*Omni*, July 1982; collected in *Burning Chrome*, Arbor House, 1986) — the same story
  that introduced the word *cyberspace* — and then in ***Neuromancer*** (Ace Books, 1984,
  ISBN 0-441-56959-5).

## Citations (short attributed quotes)

The canonical line, from "Burning Chrome" (1982), narrator Automatic Jack describing his
partner Bobby Quine:

> "Bobby was a cowboy, and ice was the nature of his game, ice from ICE, Intrusion
> Countermeasures Electronics."

In *Neuromancer* (1984) the concept deepens into **black ICE** — countermeasures that
don't merely block an intruder but kill: lethal feedback through the intruder's own deck.
*Count Zero* (1986) continues the usage. In the novels, ICE is visualized in cyberspace as
walls of ice, stone, or metal around data; black ICE is the wall that strikes back — which
is exactly the posture BANKON's ICE takes toward the wallet: passive wall (thermal/radio
gates) plus active countermeasures (boot/ban, evidence trail).

**On page numbers:** exact page numbers differ across the many editions (Omni magazine
printing, the 1986 Arbor House / 1987 Ace collection of *Burning Chrome*, and the numerous
*Neuromancer* printings), so citations here are given by story/novel + year + first-edition
publisher, which is stable. Complete paragraphs and pages of Gibson's fiction are **not
reproduced here** — the texts are under copyright; the short quotes above are used with
attribution for identification and commentary. For the full passages, see the books
themselves (worth owning).
